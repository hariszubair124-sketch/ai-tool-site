Snyk report: how enterprises are adopting agentic AI

Snyk's Agentic AI Adoption report shows enterprises moving from single models to multi-agent systems, and the security gaps that follow.

5 August 20265 min readBy Web Online Tools Editorial

In an era defined by rapid technological advancement, artificial intelligence continues to reshape the landscape of business operations. Today, August 5, 2026, a groundbreaking report from Snyk, a leader in developer security, sheds critical light on a transformative trend: the burgeoning adoption of Agentic AI architectures within enterprises. This comprehensive study reveals that an organization's actual AI footprint is often three times larger than its simple inventory of AI models suggests, marking a significant evolution from traditional AI deployments to more complex, integrated systems.

The "State of Agentic AI Adoption" report, based on an analysis of over 3,000 enterprise environments and 1.39 million code repositories, underscores that businesses are increasingly moving beyond standalone AI models. Instead, they are building sophisticated AI systems that combine models with intelligent agents and external tools to automate intricate tasks and orchestrate workflows across diverse environments. This shift has profound implications for how organizations approach productivity, cybersecurity, and overall strategic planning in the digital age.

What is Agentic AI and Why the Shift?

Agentic AI refers to artificial intelligence systems designed not just to provide answers, but to actively pursue goals, make decisions, plan actions, and execute tasks autonomously or semi-autonomously. Unlike earlier AI iterations that often functioned as reactive tools, agentic AI systems are proactive, capable of understanding context, interacting with multiple systems, and learning from their actions to achieve defined objectives. This represents a significant leap from simple chatbots to sophisticated digital colleagues that can manage complex, multi-step tasks.

The drive towards agentic AI adoption is fueled by the promise of unprecedented levels of automation and efficiency. In the face of increasing data volumes and the demand for faster, more intelligent operations, enterprises are recognizing that standalone AI models, while powerful, often require human oversight at every step. Agentic architectures aim to reduce this human-in-the-loop requirement for routine, yet complex, processes, freeing up human talent for more strategic initiatives. This paradigm shift is being observed across various industries, from IT companies deploying vast AI volumes to sectors leveraging AI for content creation and enhanced customer experiences.

Key Findings from Snyk's "State of Agentic AI Adoption" Report

Snyk's latest report provides concrete data on the acceleration of Agentic AI adoption. A substantial 46.9% of organizations utilizing AI have already embraced agentic architectures, incorporating AI agents, Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers, or both into their operations. More than half of these enterprises have deployed the full stack, integrating AI agents with MCP infrastructure that grants them access to enterprise data, applications, services, and external tools. This enables AI to perform tasks across a much broader range of systems than ever before.

A striking discovery from the report is that when supporting components like frameworks, MCP servers, retrieval systems, vector databases, and datasets are factored in, the average enterprise AI footprint is approximately three times larger than what model inventories alone would suggest. This highlights a hidden complexity in AI deployments. Furthermore, nearly half of the companies analyzed had no declared AI models in their code repositories, indicating a heavy reliance on third-party services, packages, and tools for their AI capabilities. Conversely, 17.2% of enterprises are operating large fleets of AI models, signaling deep integration of AI across various platforms and applications.

Implications for Enterprise Productivity and Security

The widespread adoption of agentic AI architectures brings dual implications for enterprises: immense opportunities for productivity gains and new challenges in cybersecurity and governance. On the productivity front, AI agents excel at coordinating workflows, retrieving information, and carrying out actions across an enterprise's digital ecosystem, extending far beyond the capabilities of simple chat interfaces. This allows for the automation of multi-step tasks, potentially revolutionizing areas such as customer service, IT infrastructure management, and data analysis.

However, this expanded AI footprint also introduces significant security risks. The report emphasizes that enterprises need comprehensive visibility into what AI systems can do, what data they consume, what resources they can access, and how they behave in production. The increasing embedding of AI into software development means developers are working with more autonomous technology, elevating the importance of robust security protocols. As AI systems become more capable and interconnected, understanding their lineage and data sources becomes paramount for incident investigation and compliance.

Addressing Challenges: The Need for AI Governance and Visibility

The complexity of agentic AI deployments necessitates a strong focus on AI governance and visibility. With approximately half of the organizations using AI models unable to link them to their training or fine-tuning datasets, there's a clear gap in understanding how models generate their results. This lack of transparency complicates compliance efforts and makes it harder to investigate security incidents. Organizations must prioritize establishing clear guidelines for AI development, deployment, and monitoring.

Implementing effective Machine Learning Operations (MLOps) practices, coupled with robust cybersecurity frameworks, will be crucial. This includes ensuring secure data handling, managing access controls for AI agents, and continuously auditing AI system behavior. For businesses utilizing online tools, it means evaluating third-party AI services not just for their functionality, but also for their security posture and adherence to data governance standards.

What's Next for Agentic AI Adoption

The trajectory for agentic AI adoption points towards continued, rapid integration across all sectors. As highlighted by the World Bank's recent report, AI is seen as a "lifeline" for emerging economies, capable of achieving decades of progress in a short timeframe, provided foundational conditions like power, connectivity, and skills are met. The focus will increasingly be on refining AI agents to be more reliable, auditable, and secure. We can anticipate further advancements in model context protocols and orchestration frameworks, making it easier for enterprises to deploy and manage these complex systems. The ongoing dialogue around AI governance, both nationally and internationally, will play a crucial role in shaping the ethical and responsible deployment of agentic AI, ensuring that its transformative power is harnessed safely for global productivity and innovation.

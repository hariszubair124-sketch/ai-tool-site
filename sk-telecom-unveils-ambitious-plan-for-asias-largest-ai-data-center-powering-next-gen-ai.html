SK Telecom Unveils Ambitious Plan for Asia's Largest AI Data Center, Powering Next-Gen AI

Published 5 July 2026 by Web Online Tools Editorial

Discover how SK Telecom's groundbreaking 15GW AI data center project aims to transform Asia into a leading AI infrastructure hub, impacting the future of online tools and digital innovation.

Seoul, South Korea – In a landmark announcement made today, July 5, 2026, SK Telecom (SKT) has revealed its ambitious initiative to construct an AI data center with an unprecedented scale of up to 15 gigawatts (GW). This monumental project is set to position South Korea as a pivotal AI infrastructure hub for the entire Asian continent, directly addressing the soaring global demand for advanced AI computing capabilities.

The strategic move by the South Korean telecommunications giant underscores a critical global trend: the intense race to build the foundational infrastructure necessary for the next generation of artificial intelligence. As AI models become increasingly sophisticated and demand for training and inference surges, high-performance computing infrastructure has become a key determinant of national competitiveness and technological leadership.

The Vision: A 15GW AI Infrastructure Hub

SK Telecom's plan involves a multi-phased approach, with the goal of ultimately reaching a 15GW capacity for its AI data center. The initial activation of 5GW is expected to commence in stages from 2029, with the full 15GW scale to follow. The company is actively reviewing core infrastructure elements such as power supply, site selection, and operational strategies, working in conjunction with the government’s "AI G3" strategy, which aims to establish Korea among the world's top three AI powers alongside the U.S. and China.

This endeavor will leverage the comprehensive capabilities of the SK Group, with various affiliates contributing their strengths across semiconductors, energy solutions, and data center construction and operation. Such a full-stack approach is critical for managing the immense complexity and cost associated with developing infrastructure of this magnitude. For perspective, constructing a typical 1GW-class AI data center can necessitate a project cost approaching KRW 70 trillion, implying a truly staggering investment for a 15GW facility. This financial commitment is expected to be met through a combination of the company's own investment, strategic partner investments, long-term customer contracts, and project financing.

Addressing the Global AI Computing Shortage

The timing of SKT's announcement is highly pertinent, given the intensifying global scramble for AI computing resources. Global consulting firm McKinsey & Company has forecasted that global data center demand is projected to grow by 19–22% annually, while supply struggles to keep pace, leading to an estimated shortfall of around 15GW in the U.S. alone by 2030. This shortage highlights the urgent need for expansive new AI data centers worldwide.

Indeed, this trend is echoed across the industry. Reports from today, July 5, 2026, indicate that U.S. tech giants such as Meta Platforms Inc., Microsoft Corp., and Oracle Corp. significantly increased their commitments for data center leases, collectively topping over $850 billion in the first quarter of 2026 alone, setting a new record for AI infrastructure spending. Oracle, in particular, holds the largest overall commitment, reportedly securing key sites linked to its contract with OpenAI. These figures underscore the universal recognition that robust AI infrastructure is the bedrock upon which future technological advancements will be built.

Driving Korea's AI G3 Strategy and Regional Development

SK Telecom’s monumental investment is not merely a corporate venture; it is deeply intertwined with South Korea’s national strategic goals. By establishing an "AI Factory" — a next-generation AI data center — SKT aims to boost domestic AI computing infrastructure, which is now seen as a crucial national strategic asset. The project is also designed to contribute to balanced regional development by linking these AI data centers with regional industries, fostering economic growth and technological diffusion beyond major metropolitan areas.

"The AI industry expects that AI data centers will serve not only as a new growth engine for Korea, but also as a national strategic asset."

This initiative aligns perfectly with the broader global recognition of AI's transformative power, where nations are actively seeking to cultivate their own AI ecosystems and secure their place in the evolving digital landscape. It's a clear signal that the future of AI is not just about algorithms and software, but about the massive physical infrastructure that underpins them.

What's Next for AI Infrastructure

Looking ahead, the commitment by SK Telecom highlights a critical trend: the global race to build superior AI computing infrastructure will only intensify. We can expect continued massive investments from governments and private corporations in high-performance computing, innovative energy solutions for sustainable data centers, and advanced cooling technologies to manage the immense heat generated by AI workloads. The focus will likely extend beyond sheer scale to include efficiency, resilience, and distributed computing models. This infrastructural boom will be the silent engine powering the continuous evolution of AI, enabling breakthroughs in everything from autonomous AI agents to multimodal systems that can see, hear, and reason simultaneously, ultimately reshaping how we interact with technology and driving further innovation in online tools.

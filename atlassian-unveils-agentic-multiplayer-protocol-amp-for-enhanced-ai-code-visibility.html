Atlassian launches AMP to track AI-written code

Atlassian announced its Agentic Multiplayer Protocol (AMP) on 7 October 2026 to show which code in a project was written by AI agents and which by people.

7 October 20265 min readBy Web Online Tools Editorial

In a significant move poised to reshape the landscape of software development, Atlassian today, October 7, 2026, announced the launch of its innovative Agentic Multiplayer Protocol (AMP). This new framework directly addresses the pressing industry challenge of AI code visibility and attribution, providing enterprises with unprecedented clarity on the origin of code in a world increasingly powered by artificial intelligence. As AI agents become integral to developer workflows, understanding who (or what) wrote what has become paramount for accountability, compliance, and effective collaboration. The introduction of AMP aims to foster a more transparent and productive environment for human and AI teams working together.

Who is Driving This Advancement in AI Code Visibility?

The push for clearer AI code visibility comes directly from Atlassian, a company synonymous with collaboration tools like Jira and Confluence. The initiative is spearheaded by Jamil Valliani, Atlassian's head of AI products, who emphasized the critical need for such solutions. According to Valliani, the lack of meaningful visibility into code origin is a substantial hurdle for most large businesses, effectively preventing further widespread adoption of AI in their development pipelines. Industry analysts largely concur, recognizing the value of increased transparency but also raising questions about the depth and utility of current offerings in tackling the often non-binary nature of human-AI hybrid code.

What is the Agentic Multiplayer Protocol (AMP)?

At its core, the Agentic Multiplayer Protocol (AMP) serves as Atlassian's foundational layer for seamless collaboration between human developers and AI agents across its platform. This protocol meticulously defines how AI agents participate in "multiplayer work" by assigning them a distinct identity, scoped authority, shared context, and, crucially, transparently traceable tasks and results. A key component of AMP is the Teamwork Graph, which meticulously indexes source code down to functions, symbols, and classes. This allows for effortless searching across repositories like Bitbucket and GitHub, while simultaneously providing full attribution and accountability, explicitly detailing what a human contributed versus what an AI agent generated across various tools, including Claude, Codex, Figma, and Rovo.

Furthermore, AMP introduces a new mode within Rovo Chat, called Rovo Work, specifically designed to handle complex, multi-step tasks under direct human oversight, ensuring that results can be reviewed and approved before implementation. For organizations with strict data residency requirements, Atlassian has also included an EU AI Inference feature, which restricts Large Language Model (LLM) processing exclusively to models hosted within the European Union.

  • Identity-based Collaboration: AI agents operate with defined identities and authorities.
  • Teamwork Graph: Indexes code for precise attribution across diverse platforms.
  • Rovo Work Mode: Facilitates human oversight for complex AI-driven tasks.
  • EU AI Inference: Ensures LLM processing adheres to European data residency requirements.

When and Where Was AMP Launched?

The Agentic Multiplayer Protocol (AMP) was officially rolled out today, October 7, 2026, by Atlassian. The company states that the core capabilities of AMP are available immediately. The protocol is designed for integration across the entire Atlassian platform, including flagship products like Jira and Confluence, as well as connected development tools such as Bitbucket and GitHub. This broad integration ensures that developers can leverage enhanced AI code visibility and attribution features within their existing workflows, without needing to switch between disparate systems. While the initial capabilities are live, Atlassian has also outlined plans for future enhancements, particularly around non-human identity (NHI) controls, which are slated to roll out "soon."

How Does Atlassian Ensure AI Code Visibility and Attribution?

Atlassian's approach to AI code visibility is multifaceted, focusing on tracing every modification and contribution back to its source. The system ensures that a clear version history is maintained, explicitly indicating whether a line of code was written by a human or generated by an AI agent. This granular level of detail is crucial for untangling the complex tapestry of hybrid codebases. For instance, if a developer accepts an AI suggestion and commits it, the system registers it as the developer's work, but the underlying history clearly shows the AI's involvement. Conversely, if an agent acts with a person's credentials, that action is recorded under the person's identity, preventing ambiguity. This intricate tracking is designed to provide comprehensive accountability, even in scenarios where human developers rewrite portions of AI-generated code, ensuring appropriate shared attribution.

"For most enterprises, this is what is holding them back from further adopting AI," stated Jamil Valliani, Atlassian's head of AI products, referring to the challenge of code origin visibility. "Attribution is easy at the edges; [but] it is hard in the middle, where most of the work now happens."

The platform executes agent-driven tasks within secure sandboxes, which can run for extended periods to achieve user-approved goals, further enhancing control and transparency.

Why This Matters for Enterprise AI Adoption and Productivity Tools

The launch of Atlassian's AMP is a significant milestone for enterprise AI adoption. By providing robust AI code visibility and attribution, Atlassian addresses a core concern that has hindered the widespread integration of AI into critical business operations: trust and accountability. Enterprises need to understand and verify the provenance of their code for various reasons, including intellectual property rights, debugging efficiency, security audits, and regulatory compliance. Without clear attribution, organizations face substantial risks when deploying AI-generated solutions.

For users of web-based productivity tools and software development platforms, AMP means greater confidence in leveraging AI. It streamlines collaborative workflows by removing ambiguity about contributions, allowing teams to focus on innovation rather than disentangling code origins. This enhanced clarity empowers developers to accept AI suggestions, integrate agent-driven tasks, and scale their use of artificial intelligence responsibly. As AI agents gain the capability to perform more autonomous actions, tools like AMP become indispensable for maintaining human oversight and ensuring ethical development practices.

What's Next for AI Code Visibility and Atlassian?

The introduction of the Agentic Multiplayer Protocol marks a crucial step in the evolution of human-AI collaboration in software development. Atlassian's commitment to advancing AI code visibility suggests a future where the lines between human and machine contributions, while blended, remain transparently traceable. The upcoming rollout of non-human identity (NHI) capabilities will further enhance control and auditing of AI agent access and actions, critical for advanced enterprise deployments. This focus on responsible AI development and clear attribution is likely to become an industry standard, pushing other platform providers to adopt similar transparency measures. For webonlinetools.com users, this means increasingly sophisticated and trustworthy AI-powered tools that boost productivity while maintaining robust oversight and accountability. As AI continues to integrate more deeply into our digital lives, solutions that provide clarity and control will be essential for harnessing its full potential safely and effectively.

CrowdStrike Boosts AI Security: Kill Switch & Dreadnode Acquisition

CrowdStrike introduced an AI Kill Switch for rogue agents and announced the acquisition of Dreadnode for automated AI security testing on 3 November 2026.

3 November 20264 min readBy Web Online Tools Editorial

CrowdStrike, a global cybersecurity leader, announced two significant advancements in AI security today, 3 November 2026 [1][2]. The company introduced an 'AI Kill Switch' designed to give human operators control over rogue AI agents and also revealed a definitive agreement to acquire Dreadnode, a specialist in automated AI security testing [1][2].

What did CrowdStrike announce?

CrowdStrike unveiled the 'AI Kill Switch' as part of its Falcon Guardian platform, providing security teams with ultimate human control over AI agents [1]. This new feature allows defenders to terminate an agent that deviates from its intended behavior, block it from relaunching, and cut off its network access [1]. According to George Kurtz, CEO and founder of CrowdStrike, "AI safety is how you get the full benefit of AI. When you deploy agents with a kill switch, you stay in control," [1].

In a separate announcement, CrowdStrike confirmed its plan to acquire Dreadnode, a company focused on automated AI security testing [2]. This acquisition aims to extend the Falcon platform by continuously testing AI models, applications, and agents for security weaknesses before they are deployed into production environments [2]. Kurtz noted that "Enterprises are deploying AI faster than they can prove it is secure. ...With Dreadnode, CrowdStrike will give every enterprise the power to trust AI and deploy faster, with confidence." [2].

How does the AI Kill Switch work?

The AI Kill Switch is designed to provide security decision-makers with final control in cases where an AI agent goes rogue [1]. It allows for the termination of a running agent at its root, where it operates [1]. The Falcon sensor, which already functions on endpoints, will stop the agent from executing, initially at the Gateway and soon directly on the endpoint [1]. This prevents a malicious or misbehaving agent from continuing to run while security teams investigate [1].

Beyond termination, the system also blocks the agent from relaunching on the targeted host or host group using Falcon Guardian policy [1]. Additionally, the agent loses its enterprise network access [1]. All these actions are triggered by an authorized administrator, and every action is recorded in an audit trail, ensuring accountability [1]. Rick Orloff, CISO at Everpure, commented, "We want our teams to use AI agents. If one goes off course, the AI Kill Switch gives our security team the final say," [1]. The AI Kill Switch is available today [1].

Why is automated AI security testing needed?

AI agents operate with system-level access across various environments, including endpoints, cloud, and SaaS [1]. They can execute multiple steps, encounter obstacles, and perform unauthorized actions even without an adversary involved [1]. Enterprises require assurance that they can maintain control over these agents to deploy them at scale [1].

Traditional security testing methods, which typically apply to code that behaves consistently, are not suitable for AI [2]. AI models and agents are probabilistic, meaning their behavior can vary [2]. They are vulnerable to manipulation through techniques like jailbreaking or prompt injection, and can fail in ways that conventional testing is not designed to detect [2]. These weaknesses often reside within the model and the agent’s reasoning loop, a layer that traditional testing does not reach [2]. Identifying and resolving these AI weaknesses must occur at the same rapid pace at which AI is being deployed [2]. Brad Palm, CEO of Dreadnode, stated that "AI fails in ways traditional testing was never designed to catch," [2].

What will the Dreadnode acquisition bring?

The acquisition of Dreadnode will integrate automated AI security testing capabilities into CrowdStrike’s Falcon platform [2]. Dreadnode's technology is designed to extend the Falcon platform into the development and testing phases of AI [2]. It will enable continuous testing of every AI model, application, and agent for security vulnerabilities before they are put into production [2].

The new capabilities will allow users to describe what to test in plain language [2]. Dreadnode runs over 100 known AI attack strategies, including jailbreaks, prompt injection, and multi-turn and agent-specific attacks, against the various components that power enterprise AI [2]. It also scans AI models, model repositories, and components for vulnerabilities [2]. The system ranks findings by severity and maps them to established security frameworks such as OWASP Top 10 for LLMs, OWASP Agentic Security, MITRE ATLAS, and NIST AI RMF [2]. It provides exportable reports to aid teams in making deployment decisions [2]. The platform also supports human-in-the-loop review, allowing operators to reclassify findings, adjust severity, and document their reasoning [2]. Dreadnode findings will also inform Falcon Guardian policy recommendations and strengthen CrowdStrike's SafeMind models [2]. The Dreadnode team will join CrowdStrike's Cyber Superintelligence Lab, enhancing the company's AI security research [2]. The acquisition is expected to close during CrowdStrike’s fourth quarter of FY27, with Automated AI Security Testing generally available in the same quarter [2]. As Palm noted, "As part of CrowdStrike, we will bring Automated AI Security Testing to every enterprise, helping teams confidently find and fix weaknesses." [2].

Satya Nadella testifies in Musk's lawsuit against OpenAI

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella testified in Elon Musk's lawsuit over OpenAI's move from non-profit to commercial company.

11 May 20265 min readBy Web Online Tools Editorial

The high-stakes legal battle between Elon Musk and OpenAI reached a pivotal moment today, May 11, 2026, as Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella took the stand in a federal court in Oakland, California. The lawsuit, initiated by Musk, alleges that OpenAI betrayed its founding non-profit mission by transitioning into a for-profit entity, fundamentally altering the trajectory of artificial intelligence development. Nadella's testimony is expected to shed light on Microsoft's significant financial and strategic involvement in OpenAI's evolution, marking a critical juncture in the ongoing debate over the ethics and commercialization of AI.

Who is Involved in the OpenAI Lawsuit?

At the center of this contentious legal saga are three powerful entities and their influential leaders: Elon Musk, co-founder of OpenAI; OpenAI itself, represented by CEO Sam Altman and co-founder Greg Brockman; and Microsoft, a primary investor, led by CEO Satya Nadella. Musk, the billionaire entrepreneur behind Tesla and SpaceX, was a co-founder of OpenAI in 2015, contributing significant initial funding with the vision of developing AI for the benefit of humanity. His lawsuit claims a departure from this original ethos. OpenAI, now a leader in generative AI with products like ChatGPT, transitioned to a "capped-profit" model in 2019, drawing substantial investment from Microsoft.

Microsoft's involvement is crucial, as its deep pockets and cloud computing resources (Azure) have been instrumental in OpenAI's growth and technological advancements. Nadella's testimony today focuses on explaining the emails and discussions that underpinned Microsoft's funding, particularly how the company supported OpenAI's shift from a philanthropic organization to an AI giant seeking profitability.

What is the Core of the Dispute: AI's Mission and Commercialization?

The fundamental issue at the heart of the lawsuit is OpenAI's alleged abandonment of its original non-profit mission. Elon Musk argues that OpenAI was founded on the principle of developing artificial general intelligence (AGI) as an open-source technology for the benefit of all, preventing it from being controlled by a single corporation. He asserts that his initial donations, reportedly totaling $38 million, were made under this premise.

Musk's legal team is using internal documents, including private journal entries by OpenAI co-founder Greg Brockman, to illustrate discussions about the company's financial future and a shift towards a profit-driven structure. These documents reportedly detail aspirations for significant personal net worth linked to a high company valuation, which Musk's lawyers contend deviates sharply from a non-profit objective. OpenAI, conversely, maintains that Musk voluntarily left the company in 2018 after failing to gain majority control and has since become a direct competitor with his own AI venture, xAI.

When and Where the Legal Drama Unfolds

The trial is taking place in a federal court in Oakland, California. Today, May 11, 2026, marks Satya Nadella's testimony. The lawsuit's origins trace back to OpenAI's founding in 2015 and Musk's subsequent departure in 2018. Internal documents, initially filed under seal in October 2025, were unsealed in January 2026, providing critical evidence for the prosecution. The proceedings have already seen dramatic testimony, including from Greg Brockman last week, and OpenAI CEO Sam Altman is expected to face questioning later this week, likely on May 12 or 13.

Why This Lawsuit Matters for the Future of AI

This lawsuit extends far beyond a corporate dispute; it cuts to the core of how artificial intelligence will be developed, governed, and deployed in the future. The outcome could significantly impact the balance between open-source AI development and commercial interests, influencing intellectual property rights, data utilization, and ethical guidelines across the tech industry. For webonlinetools.com users, this debate underscores the importance of transparent and responsible AI development, especially as AI-powered tools become increasingly integrated into daily productivity and creative workflows.

  • Ethical AI Development: The case highlights concerns about AI safety, control, and ensuring that advanced AI benefits society broadly rather than serving narrow commercial agendas.
  • Corporate Governance and Transparency: It raises questions about the obligations of founding members and investors when a company's mission evolves, particularly in rapidly advancing fields like AI.
  • Impact on Innovation: Depending on the verdict, it could set precedents for how future AI research is funded and commercialized, potentially affecting both established tech giants and nascent AI startups.

The tension between building powerful AI and ensuring it remains aligned with societal benefit is a central theme. As AI capabilities grow, the stakes for how these technologies are managed become even higher, making this lawsuit a landmark case for the entire industry.

How the Trial is Unfolding

The trial involves a meticulous examination of internal communications, financial agreements, and the shifting strategic objectives within OpenAI. Musk's lawyers are attempting to demonstrate that Microsoft, by investing heavily in OpenAI in 2019, was aware it was facilitating a departure from the foundation's original non-profit charter. Recently disclosed Microsoft emails from January 2018 are being presented as evidence, purportedly showing that Microsoft's significant financial commitment solidified once a profit-making pathway for OpenAI became clear. These emails reportedly show Nadella consulting executives about discounts provided to OpenAI for using Microsoft's Azure cloud-computing platform.

The legal team for Musk is also utilizing internal AI chatbot prompts and other digital records as part of the discovery process, demonstrating the increasing role of digital forensics in corporate litigation. This deep dive into past communications and financial dealings aims to establish a pattern of conduct that, according to Musk, fundamentally betrayed the spirit of OpenAI's inception.

What's Next for OpenAI and the AI Industry?

Following Satya Nadella's testimony, the spotlight will turn to OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, whose questioning is anticipated later this week. An "advisory" jury is expected to deliver a verdict on any actual wrongdoing by the week of May 18, with Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers issuing the final ruling on liability and remedies after considering the jury's opinion.

Regardless of the legal outcome, this lawsuit has already brought unprecedented scrutiny to the internal workings and foundational principles of a leading AI developer. It serves as a stark reminder for all companies working with artificial intelligence, including those developing free online tools for productivity and content generation, to consider the long-term ethical implications of their business models and technological advancements. The resolution of this case could redefine corporate responsibility in the age of advanced AI and significantly influence the future direction of artificial intelligence governance globally.

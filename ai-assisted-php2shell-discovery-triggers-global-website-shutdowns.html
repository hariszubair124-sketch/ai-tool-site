AI-assisted PHP2Shell discovery triggers global website shutdowns

AI-assisted discovery of 'PHP2Shell' vulnerabilities in PHP programming language leads to urgent global patching efforts and website shutdowns on 21 October 202

22 October 20266 min readBy Web Online Tools Editorial

The PHP Foundation disclosed two critical vulnerabilities, dubbed 'PHP2Shell,' on Wednesday, October 21, 2026, triggering an urgent global patching push for PHP-based websites [1]. These significant flaws, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, were identified with the assistance of an artificial intelligence system [1][3].

What are the 'PHP2Shell' vulnerabilities?

The 'PHP2Shell' vulnerabilities refer to two critical remote code execution (RCE) flaws, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, that affect the core PHP engine itself [2][5]. These vulnerabilities are rated 9.2 under CVSS v4.0, indicating their severe nature [2]. They are memory-safety issues stemming from how PHP handles request memory during initialization, error handling, and cleanup processes [2][5].

Specifically, CVE-2026-109618 is an out-of-memory (OOM) related vulnerability involving partially initialized request memory [2]. Security firm Wiz identified a use-after-free (UAF) flaw in the session upload-progress unserializer, along with a related issue that could disclose PHP worker memory [2][5]. The second vulnerability, CVE-2026-109619, is a stale-state flaw where PHP globals retain references to freed request memory [2]. This involves a UAF in fatal-error backtraces and a separate OPcache type-confusion flaw [2][5].

Both vulnerabilities mean an attacker can remotely run commands on a server operating vulnerable PHP code over the internet, without requiring a password or legitimate account access [3][4][5]. Wiz demonstrated unauthenticated RCE against default installations of popular content management systems like WordPress, Drupal, and Joomla [2][5]. The underlying flaws have existed for at least a decade, with the affected range stretching back to PHP 5.4 [3][4][5][6]. PHP released fixed versions on October 21, 2026, including PHP 8.2.35, 8.3.36, 8.4.27, and 8.5.12 [2].

How did AI assist in the discovery?

The discovery of 'PHP2Shell' marks a significant moment, transforming it from a routine security bulletin into an AI story [1]. An organization named Calif identified the flaws with the assistance of an AI model referred to as GPT-5.6 Sol [1]. Notably, the security firm Wiz independently discovered and reported both vulnerabilities on its own, without relying on Calif's findings [1]. This dual-track discovery suggests the bugs were accessible through multiple investigative approaches, bolstering confidence in their reality [1].

Researchers at Calif, collaborating with OpenAI, reported the first flaw on August 11, while Wiz, a security firm owned by Alphabet, reported the second vulnerability on August 24 [3]. Wiz stated that these findings "rank among the most severe vulnerabilities discovered in PHP" and that proving their exploitability required a complex chain of steps "at the edge of what human researchers could practically achieve" [3]. The official Wiz disclosure confirmed that frontier AI models were used to chain these intricate memory-safety flaws into functional exploits [5].

AI tools allow researchers to uncover vulnerabilities in software by testing a much greater number of attacks than human programmers could alone [3][6]. Robert McMillan, a Wall Street Journal reporter, explained that while humans might have suspected "something weird was going on," they struggled to develop an exploit [6]. He noted that an AI can perform such a "menial, complex, long-term task much more quickly" than a human [6]. OpenAI published its own account of the coordinated disclosure process, highlighting how AI-assisted research can integrate with established vulnerability disclosure norms [1]. OpenAI and Google are listed as contributors to the response, alongside Wiz and Cloudflare [1].

Why is the impact so widespread?

The widespread use of PHP is the core reason for the extensive impact of these vulnerabilities [1]. PHP is an open-source programming language that powers a vast portion of the internet, with reports indicating it runs as much as 70% of the world's websites and web applications [1][6]. This includes popular frameworks like WordPress, Laravel, Drupal, and Joomla, as well as large e-commerce platforms, government portals, and numerous sites running on shared hosting environments [1][2][5][6].

When a critical bug affects the PHP interpreter itself, every application built on top of it inherits the risk, regardless of the specific hosting panel or control software a site owner uses [1]. The vulnerabilities affect PHP as a language, rather than a particular website application, meaning even sites with secure application code may be vulnerable depending on the PHP runtime, server configuration, and reachable execution paths [2].

In response to the disclosure, authorities across Europe, Asia, and other continents have temporarily taken some government websites offline as a preventative measure [3]. On Wednesday, the United States National Security Agency (NSA) issued a rare public warning, advising organizations to be prepared for an update and to take websites offline if immediate patching was not possible [3]. Germany's Federal Ministry for Digital Transformation and Government Modernisation and France’s Ministry of the Interior both deactivated official websites [3]. Authorities in Belgium, the Netherlands, Finland, and the Czech Republic also shut down websites to apply the security patch [3]. Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi used social media to warn about the PHP risk [3].

Internet scanning companies have highlighted the scale of exposure. Censys reported that approximately 1.5 million of the 2.6 million hosts it examined were running a version of PHP without the necessary fix [1]. Modat, another scanning service, counted 4,384,302 PHP services on 1,886,612 unique hosts [2]. The FBI's cyber threat division acknowledged the vulnerabilities, stating they expose websites and applications to interference from cybercriminals who could remotely insert malware into PHP's source code [6].

What is the current status and what should users do?

The PHP Foundation has issued urgent public guidance, stating plainly: "If you run PHP, it is imperative that you update now" [1][6]. PHP maintainers separately reinforced this message, encouraging users to "update immediately" [1]. Fixed versions for supported PHP 8 branches were released on October 21, 2026 [2][5]. However, older, unsupported PHP branches, such as PHP 5 and PHP 7, are not covered by these specific fixes and may never receive an official patch [2][5]. For systems running these older versions, upgrading, isolating, or shielding them from the internet is considered the only viable option [5].

While there are reports of emerging exploitation attempts targeting 'PHP2Shell,' publicly available evidence remains insufficient to establish the full extent of successful exploitation or confirmed compromises [1][2]. Patchstack received reports of multiple active exploitation incidents from hosting partners by 07:34 UTC on October 21, ahead of PHP's security release [2]. Cloudflare CTO Dane Knecht also reported observing early attacks and subsequently deployed web application firewall (WAF) protections against known exploit vectors [2]. Numerous public GitHub repositories have emerged, claiming to provide proof-of-concept (PoC) exploits, though their effectiveness and relationship to the confirmed vulnerabilities vary, with some relying on invalidated theories [2].

For regular internet users, simply visiting a vulnerable PHP website does not necessarily mean their device will be compromised [6]. The primary risk is that attackers may exploit a website that stores personal information, potentially extracting data such as email addresses, physical addresses, names, or dates of birth [6]. Users should remain extra cautious about suspicious messages, as the type and extent of data hackers may have stolen through this vulnerability are currently unknown [6]. Organizations that cannot immediately patch their sites are advised to temporarily take them offline [3][6]. A suggested stopgap measure, though unverified and not a substitute for patching, is setting session.upload_progress.enabled = Off in php.ini [5]. Monitoring for post-exploitation activity, such as PHP processes spawning shells or downloading tools, is also recommended [5].

What are the broader implications of AI in cybersecurity?

The 'PHP2Shell' incident represents a meaningful shift in how critical PHP vulnerabilities are typically discovered [1]. Historically, PHP-core bugs were found through manual code audits, fuzzing campaigns, or incident response after a breach [1]. An AI-assisted discovery, publicly credited alongside a major PHP maintainer organization, establishes a new pattern that security teams and web hosting providers are closely observing as a preview of future vulnerability research [1].

Andrés Guerrero-Saade, Vice President of intelligence and security research at SentinelOne, commented that this incident illustrates "how AI is accelerating the ability of vulnerability researchers" [6]. However, he also noted that AI simultaneously boosts the capacity of malicious actors to exploit security flaws [6]. The crucial time window between a vulnerability being detected and a fix being released, often referred to as the "exposure window," has been shortened by AI, enabling cybercriminals to move much faster than in the past [6].

The PHP Foundation warned that AI would make such security weaknesses easier to find, calling on tech companies to mount a coordinated response to prevent major disruptions to online services [3]. In a statement, the foundation declared, "Tools now find bugs faster than ever, for defenders and attackers alike, and the number of reports will keep growing" [3]. Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella emphasized the importance of developing software with increasingly robust security measures, describing the discovery as "a long day and a long night" for IT and security professionals [6].

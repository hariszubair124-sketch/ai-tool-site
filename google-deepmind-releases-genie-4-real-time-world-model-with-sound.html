Google DeepMind releases Genie 4: Real-time world model with sound

Google DeepMind introduced Genie 4, a real-time interactive world model generating sound and simulating physics, on 27 October 2026.

29 October 20264 min readBy Web Online Tools Editorial

Google DeepMind has introduced Genie 4, an interactive world model capable of generating explorable virtual environments in real time. The announcement, made on 27 October 2026, marks the release of a model that also creates sound to match its scenes [1][2].

What is Genie 4?

Genie 4 is described as Google DeepMind's most realistic interactive world model to date [2]. At its core, a world model is defined as an artificial intelligence that generates an explorable virtual environment from a text description, reacting in real time [2]. This latest iteration allows users to navigate through its generated worlds using a keyboard, providing a direct and immersive interaction [1]. Beyond visual generation, the model integrates a comprehensive auditory experience, creating music, ambient sound, and spoken dialogue that accompanies the video output [1]. This combination aims to deliver a richer, more engaging simulated reality [1].

The model is designed for high interactivity, enabling users to steer the virtual environment dynamically through text prompts [1]. These prompts offer granular control, allowing changes to elements such as weather conditions, camera angles, or even the characters present within the scene [1]. For instance, a user could command the model to "change to a rainy day" or "add a new character," and the environment would adapt accordingly [1]. A significant advancement in this version is its ability to simulate complex physics, including phenomena like collisions and aerodynamics [1]. This capability adds a layer of realism and dynamic interaction, making the virtual worlds feel more tangible and responsive to user actions [1]. The model's capacity to change the world "on the fly with text instructions" highlights its real-time adaptability [2].

How does the model function?

The sophisticated simulation capabilities of Genie 4 are powered by Gemini 3.8 Flash, which serves as an intelligent guiding agent [1][2]. This agent plays a crucial role by "watching the scene, breaks goals into steps and checks each one" [1]. This structured, goal-oriented approach helps the model maintain coherence and responsiveness, ensuring that the interactive environment behaves logically and consistently with user inputs [1]. The integration of Gemini 3.8 Flash allows for a more intelligent interpretation of prompts and a more stable progression of simulated events [1].

Each interactive experience generated by the model is designed to last approximately two minutes [2]. This duration represents a notable improvement, being "twice as long as with Genie 3," its predecessor [2]. While this extension allows for more prolonged engagement, Google has indicated that "details can still drift in longer runs" [2], suggesting ongoing development to enhance long-term consistency. To maintain transparency and provide clear identification of AI-generated content, all output produced by the model carries SynthID watermarks [1]. These watermarks serve as a clear indicator of the content's origin, which is an important consideration for AI-generated media [1].

Who can access it, and when?

Currently, Genie 4 is being rolled out as a "research preview" [1][2]. Access during this initial phase is limited to "trusted testers" who are participating in the evaluation of the model [1][2]. This controlled release allows DeepMind to gather feedback and refine the technology before wider deployment [1].

Looking ahead, the model is slated to become more broadly available [1][2]. Google AI Ultra subscribers will be among the first to gain access, with interested individuals able to "sign up on the Project Genie waitlist" [2]. Furthermore, Genie 4 is expected to be integrated into the Gemini API and AI Studio, making it accessible to "early partners" [1]. According to Logan Kilpatrick, a figure associated with the announcement, the model is also "coming to the API" [2], reinforcing its planned availability for developers and businesses looking to incorporate its capabilities into their own applications [1].

What are the implications of this technology?

The introduction of Genie 4 signifies a notable advancement in artificial intelligence's capacity to directly produce sophisticated research results and simulated worlds . By combining real-time visual generation with integrated sound and physics simulation, the model offers a comprehensive and deeply interactive virtual experience [1]. The ability for users to "walk through" these environments with a keyboard and "steer with text prompts" to modify various aspects like weather or characters could unlock new possibilities across diverse fields [1].

This technology holds potential for creative expression, enabling artists and storytellers to rapidly prototype and iterate on immersive narratives [1]. In fields like game development or virtual reality, it could streamline the creation of dynamic environments that respond intelligently to user input [1]. The model's capacity to generate not just visuals but also a full auditory experience, including music, ambient sounds, and dialogue, significantly enhances the immersion for users, making the virtual worlds feel more alive [1]. The underlying guiding agent, Gemini 3.8 Flash, demonstrates an advanced level of AI control over complex, dynamic environments, suggesting a future where AI can manage intricate simulations with greater autonomy [1]. While currently in a research preview phase, the planned future availability through Google AI Ultra and the Gemini API suggests a broader application for developers and users in the near future, potentially making these advanced simulation capabilities more widely accessible [1][2]. The emphasis on independent verification, as highlighted in a broader industry brief concerning AI outputs, underscores the importance of careful scrutiny for such advanced AI systems .

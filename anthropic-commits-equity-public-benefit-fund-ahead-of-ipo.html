Anthropic Commits Equity: Public Benefit Fund Ahead of IPO

Anthropic commits 5% of its equity to a new Public Benefit Fund ahead of its November IPO, aiming to balance public interests with profitability.

20 October 20265 min readBy Web Online Tools Editorial

Anthropic, the creator of Claude models, has committed 5% of its equity to a new Public Benefit Fund [1][2]. This move comes as the company prepares for its initial public offering (IPO) in November [1].

What did Anthropic announce?

Anthropic will commit 5% of its shares to a new Public Benefit Fund LLC [1][2]. This commitment is subject to stockholder approval [1]. An additional 2% of shares could be added to the fund at the board's discretion for every 50% rise in the company's value above a yet-to-be-disclosed baseline [1][2]. The company is heading toward a November IPO [1], positioning itself as a public benefit corporation [2]. This legal structure requires management to balance broader public interests with profitability goals [2]. The flotation is anticipated to be one of the biggest IPOs in history [2]. While the company's valuation could reach roughly $2 trillion, Reuters exclusively reported last week that some investors are weighing a lower valuation of around $1.5 trillion [2]. Based on these figures, a 5% stake in the fund would be valued in a range of $75 billion to $100 billion [2].

How will the Public Benefit Fund work?

The proposed fund would be established as a wholly owned limited liability company [2]. It is designed to give the company more flexibility and scale to make donations for the benefit of humanity [2]. The fund could support various initiatives, including direct distributions to specific populations [2]. It will also focus on efforts to address AI’s impact on the labor market [2], building on a previously announced $200 million commitment to study and address labor market disruption [2]. The AI giant stated that none of the equity held by its fund would be used for lobbying or political giving, either in the US or abroad [2]. This decision comes at a time when US policymakers are considering greater oversight of AI, and companies are increasing efforts to shape how the technology is regulated [2].

Why is Anthropic doing this?

The company has positioned itself as a "safety-first AI lab" [2]. It has previously stated that AI could pose "catastrophic or existential risks to humanity" [2], highlighting its focus on responsible development. This initiative is part of its broader approach to sharing some of the "spoils of the AI boom" [2] and addressing the societal implications of artificial intelligence. Public scrutiny over the burgeoning AI sector has deepened due to security breaches involving AI systems and warnings from industry leaders that runaway development could pose severe risks [2]. There is also growing public anxiety over potential job losses [2]. Global companies have already cut thousands of jobs after investing heavily in AI, with many, primarily tech firms, pointing to higher efficiencies from the technology's rapid adoption [2]. The company's commitment to the Public Benefit Fund, combined with its co-founders’ pledge to donate 80% of their equity and an employee donation matching program, means that roughly 20% of Anthropic’s shares are now dedicated to public benefit [2]. This comprehensive approach underscores its dedication to balancing innovation with societal well-being.

How does this compare to other philanthropic efforts?

Anthropic's strategy outlines a "sharply different blueprint" for philanthropy compared to the approach taken by Elon Musk [2]. Mr. Musk pledged on Monday to give one SpaceX share to "every kid in America," arguing for such direct distributions as the AI sector faces public scrutiny [2]. "Next year, I will give one of my SpaceX shares to every kid in America. This will be the first of many," Mr. Musk, who runs Anthropic rival SpaceXSI, said in a post early on Monday [2]. He held approximately 6.42 billion shares in the company as of June [2]. Mr. Musk's share giveaway would be worth roughly $11 billion at current share prices, based on US census figures indicating 72 million children in America [2]. This figure would be significantly dwarfed by the potential value of Anthropic's 5% stake, which is estimated to be between $75 billion and $100 billion [2]. Mr. Musk emphasized his philosophy, stating, "Giving shares, which are stakes in the future economy, directly to the people is the way" [2].

Mr. Musk's announcement follows the White House's launch of "Trump Accounts" months prior, a program designed to provide US citizens born between 2025 and 2028 with a government-funded investment account of $1,000 that families can build upon [2]. Several prominent US companies, including Dell, Visa, Micron, and Delta Air Lines, have already pledged support for this program through employer matches or additional funding [2]. President Donald Trump expressed gratitude on Truth Social for Mr. Musk's "incredible generosity" in giving SpaceX stock through Trump Accounts [2]. Earlier this year, SpaceX President Gwynne Shotwell and her husband also pledged a share of their SpaceX stock to more than 2 million children under the Trump initiative [2]. Robert Pavlik, senior portfolio manager at Dakota Wealth Management, offered a skeptical view of Mr. Musk's announcement, saying, "It feels like a publicity stunt… Will it create positive public sentiment? Perhaps." [2] He also noted, "That’s a lot of shares… I’m sure somebody will be happy to take them" [2]. Michael Dell and his wife Susan have committed $6.25 billion in cash to Trump Accounts, specifically targeting underprivileged children [2]. In contrast, the value of Mr. Musk's contribution will fluctuate with SpaceX’s often volatile share performance [2]. Most of Mr. Musk's philanthropic endeavors have historically been channeled through the Musk Foundation, which reportedly holds more than $14 billion in assets [2]. For broader context, Berkshire Hathaway Chairman Emeritus Warren Buffett is reported to have given away $60 billion to date, with the majority of his remaining roughly $150 billion fortune also designated for philanthropy [2]. The Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation, partly fueled by Mr. Buffett's donations, has distributed over $100 billion since its establishment in 2000 [2].

What's next for the company?

The company is moving forward with its November IPO [1]. It will continue to operate as a public benefit corporation, balancing public interests with profit [2]. The Public Benefit Fund's specific initiatives will likely unfold after the IPO and stockholder approval [1][2]. The company's commitment to addressing AI's impact on the labor market and other societal benefits will remain a central focus [2].

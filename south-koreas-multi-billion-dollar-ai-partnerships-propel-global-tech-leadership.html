South Korea signs AI deals with Nvidia, Samsung and Broadcom

South Korea signed multi-billion-dollar AI partnerships with Nvidia, Samsung and Broadcom as part of its national AI strategy.

25 July 20264 min readBy Web Online Tools Editorial

San Francisco witnessed a pivotal moment in the global artificial intelligence landscape this week as South Korean President Lee Jae Myung hosted an influential AI summit, forging unprecedented multi-billion dollar partnerships with leading technology companies. The initiative, announced on July 24-25, 2026, underscores South Korea's determined ambition to solidify its position as a global AI powerhouse and drive the next wave of innovation across diverse sectors, from advanced semiconductors to real-world physical AI applications. This strategic move is set to dramatically influence the foundational infrastructure powering everything from complex online tools to enterprise-level productivity solutions.

A Strategic Summit in San Francisco

The high-profile AI summit, held in San Francisco, brought together an impressive roster of technology titans. President Lee Jae Myung convened with the chief executives of global innovators such as Nvidia (Jensen Huang), OpenAI (Sam Altman), Anthropic (Dario Amodei), and Broadcom (Hock Tan). Crucially, top South Korean business figures, including Samsung Electronics Executive Chairman Jay Y. Lee, SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won, Hyundai Motor Group Executive Chair Euisun Chung, and Naver founder Lee Hae-jin, were also in attendance, signaling a unified national commitment to AI advancement.

During his keynote address, President Lee unveiled the "San Francisco AI Declaration," a comprehensive document outlining South Korea's vision for AI and its strong emphasis on international technological cooperation with the United States. He articulated a clear goal: "South Korea will make the leap as a key nation in the global AI supply chain," a declaration that resonated with the scale of the announced collaborations.

Unpacking the Multi-Billion Dollar Collaborations

The summit yielded several monumental agreements designed to bolster South Korea's AI infrastructure and capabilities. These partnerships represent a significant financial commitment and a deep integration of technological expertise, promising to accelerate AI development at an unprecedented pace.

Nvidia and SK Group: Powering AI Factories and Next-Gen Memory

One of the most impactful announcements was the more than $500-billion partnership between Nvidia and SK Group. This comprehensive initiative focuses on establishing critical AI infrastructure to meet the surging global demand for compute power. As part of this agreement, SK Telecom plans to construct a massive 2-gigawatt NVIDIA Vera Rubin DSX AI Factory, anticipated to be online by 2027. This state-of-the-art data center will leverage NVIDIA's advanced accelerated computing platforms, powered by SK Hynix's HBM4 high-bandwidth memory chips. The collaboration also includes a long-term partnership between Nvidia and SK Hynix to secure and co-develop next-generation AI memory, which is crucial for handling the immense data requirements of modern AI models.

Samsung Electronics and Broadcom: Foundry and Memory Leadership

Samsung Electronics and Broadcom formalized a strategic collaboration valued at over $200 billion over the next five years, extending through 2030. This partnership will see Samsung supplying industry-leading memory solutions, including High Bandwidth Memory (HBM), to support Broadcom's next-generation AI accelerators. Furthermore, the collaboration will extend to foundry technologies, with a focus on Samsung's cutting-edge 2-nanometer and below process technologies for Broadcom's products, including Wireless Broadband Communications (WBC) solutions. Advanced packaging technologies, built on Samsung's 2nm process, such as 2.3D and 2.5D integration, are also part of this extensive agreement.

Hyundai Motor Group's Physical AI Vision

Hyundai Motor Group Executive Chair Euisun Chung used the San Francisco AI Summit to articulate the group's "Physical AI Vision." This roadmap details how Hyundai plans to evolve beyond traditional boundaries by integrating AI into robotics, enhancing manufacturing processes through sophisticated digital twins, and advancing autonomous driving solutions. The Group is building on a prior supply agreement for 50,000 NVIDIA Blackwell GPUs and a memorandum of understanding to bolster Korea's Physical AI capabilities, including establishing a Robot Application Center and collaborations with NVIDIA's AI Technology Center.

Why These South Korea AI Partnerships Matter

These monumental partnerships signify a profound shift in the global AI landscape. Firstly, they demonstrate a concerted effort by a nation to strategically position itself at the forefront of AI development, not just as a consumer, but as a crucial provider of critical hardware and infrastructure. The sheer scale of investment — with South Korean companies pursuing memory chip supply partnerships worth a total of $950 billion with U.S. big tech firms — highlights the economic gravity of this push.

For users of web-based online tools and productivity platforms, this means a future with more powerful, efficient, and accessible AI capabilities. The advancements in high-bandwidth memory, efficient data centers, and specialized AI accelerators directly contribute to faster processing, improved performance for AI-driven applications, and potentially lower operational costs for cloud-based AI services. This infrastructure underpins the next generation of smart assistants, advanced content creation tools, and sophisticated analytical platforms that webonlinetools.com users rely on daily.

Furthermore, the focus on "Physical AI" by companies like Hyundai suggests a future where AI extends beyond digital interfaces into tangible robots and autonomous systems, promising innovations in manufacturing, logistics, and everyday life. The increasing competition and collaboration among global tech players, as seen in this summit, will likely drive innovation faster, leading to a more robust and diverse AI ecosystem.

What's Next for South Korea's AI Ambitions

The announcements from the San Francisco AI Summit mark the beginning of a new era for South Korea's role in the global AI supply chain. The coming years will see the materialization of these multi-billion dollar investments, with projects like SK Telecom's AI factory expected to commence operations by 2027. We can anticipate continued innovation in advanced memory and semiconductor manufacturing, further integration of AI into traditional industries like automotive and robotics, and an accelerated pace of AI research and development fueled by these strategic alliances. As the world moves towards more agentic AI and physical AI, South Korea's proactive stance and extensive partnerships will undoubtedly shape the technological advancements that will redefine productivity and digital experiences globally.

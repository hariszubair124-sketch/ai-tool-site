Nvidia and Synopsys use AI agents for chip design

Nvidia and Synopsys are using autonomous AI agents in chip and system design. Which engineering tasks the agents handle and the gains reported.

27 July 20265 min readBy Web Online Tools Editorial

The landscape of technology is continually evolving, and today, July 27, 2026, marks a significant moment in the advancement of artificial intelligence. Recent groundbreaking announcements from tech giants NVIDIA and Synopsys reveal a profound shift towards truly autonomous AI agents that are beginning to redefine complex engineering workflows. This development pushes AI beyond mere assistance, empowering it to independently manage intricate, multi-step tasks, setting a new benchmark for productivity across specialized and potentially general online tools.

The Dawn of Autonomous AI Engineering

The core of this transformative news centers around major collaborations unveiled at the recent 2026 DAC Chips to Systems Conference. On July 26, 2026, NVIDIA announced a substantial expansion of its Agent Toolkit, integrating powerful new libraries: PhysicsNeMo™ and CUDA-X™. These additions are specifically designed to enable software developers to build "autonomous AI engineers" with advanced AI physics skills, accelerated solvers, and quantum chemistry capabilities. This signifies a move towards AI systems that can reason, run complex simulations, and generate high-fidelity data autonomously.

Following closely, Synopsys, a leader in electronic design automation (EDA), also revealed significant advancements in agentic AI for engineering. Collaborating with NVIDIA, Synopsys has developed fully autonomous, long-running agentic capabilities for both chip design and electronics system design. These new capabilities are powered by NVIDIA Nemotron on NVIDIA's accelerated computing platform, secured by the NVIDIA OpenShell runtime, showcasing a tightly integrated ecosystem for advanced AI deployment.

Who is Driving This Innovation?

The primary architects behind these innovations are NVIDIA and Synopsys. NVIDIA, renowned for its graphics processing units (GPUs) and AI platforms, continues to be at the forefront of AI hardware and software development. Their Agent Toolkit expansion provides the foundational libraries necessary for creating sophisticated AI agents. Synopsys, a company deeply embedded in chip design and verification, is leveraging these NVIDIA technologies to build practical, deployable autonomous workflows that address real-world engineering challenges.

What Do These Autonomous AI Agents Do?

These new autonomous AI agents are designed to handle highly complex engineering tasks with minimal human intervention. NVIDIA's PhysicsNeMo libraries provide AI physics skills for training and deploying models, while CUDA-X libraries bring accelerated solvers and quantum chemistry into agentic engineering workflows. This means AI can now actively participate in the intricate processes of chip design, verification, packaging, and overall systems engineering. Synopsys specifically demonstrated a fully autonomous, long-running design verification agent capable of orchestrating an entire chip verification cycle, delivering up to 50 times faster time-to-validated RTL with an additional 20% coverage improvement. This is a monumental leap from traditional human-intensive processes.

When and Where Were These Announcements Made?

These significant technological breakthroughs were formally announced on July 26, 2026. The stage for these revelations was the prestigious 2026 DAC (Design Automation Conference) Chips to Systems Conference. NVIDIA's announcement originated from Long Beach, California, while Synopsys's related news came from Sunnyvale, California, both pivotal locations in the tech world for such high-impact disclosures.

How Autonomous AI Agents Function

The functionality of these advanced AI agents is rooted in a shift from tool-assisted workflows to goal-driven autonomous processes. Synopsys’s solution, built on its agentic AI platform and powered by AgentEngineer™ technology, features a fully autonomous, long-running orchestrator agent. This orchestrator agent uses NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra, a leading open model in agentic register-transfer level coding, to pursue coverage closure and trace root failure causes throughout development without constant human prompting. The system can interpret intent, compose workflows, and make decisions within a governed execution framework, ensuring control and reviewability.

The integration of NVIDIA's re-architected PhysicsNeMo libraries and updated CUDA-X libraries into the Agent Toolkit empowers these AI entities with specialized tools, enabling them to run simulations and generate high-fidelity data. This allows for a new class of autonomous AI engineers that can scale complex design, verification, packaging, and systems work, transforming how products are engineered, designed, and built. Essentially, the AI is given a goal and then autonomously figures out the multi-step process, tools, and simulations required to achieve it.

Why This Matters: Revolutionizing Productivity

The emergence of autonomous AI agents in engineering marks a pivotal moment for productivity. By compressing weeks of manual labor into hours of agentic execution, these advancements dramatically reduce time-to-market for complex designs and free human engineers to focus on higher-level innovation and problem-solving. This isn't just about incremental improvements; it's about a fundamental restructuring of how work is conceptualized and executed in highly technical fields.

This shift aligns with broader industry trends, as Gartner forecasts worldwide end-user spending on AI platforms and models to grow by 63% in 2026, reaching $64 billion. A key driver for this growth is the increasing scrutiny on usage efficiency, cost control, and measurable outcomes from enterprise AI budgets. Autonomous agents directly address these demands by offering unparalleled efficiency and precision, validating the significant investment in AI technologies. The "Genesis Mission," a national effort to harness AI for science, also highlights the overarching goal of doubling the productivity and impact of research and innovation through AI integration.

The Road Ahead: What's Next for Autonomous AI

The introduction of autonomous AI agents in engineering workflows is just the beginning. We can anticipate rapid advancements in the capabilities of these agents, extending to more domains and handling even greater complexity. Future developments will likely focus on enhancing their contextual understanding, improving their ability to learn from feedback, and ensuring robust safety and ethical guardrails are in place. The development of governed AI infrastructure, as seen with systems designed to ensure authority resolves before execution, will be critical for secure and reliable deployment in sensitive areas. As these technologies mature, their integration into everyday online tools will unlock new paradigms of productivity, offering unprecedented opportunities for automation and innovation across all sectors. The focus will remain on building AI that not only thinks but can also act with permission and produce reviewable outcomes, ultimately transforming how we interact with technology and accomplish our goals.
